The requested bootstrap uses tscircuit's **native `bus_lanes` preset**, implemented by `BusLanesPipelineSolver`. The installed core is now 0.0.2050; attempts 1–16 below used 0.0.2048. The current [DDR guide](https://docs.tscircuit.com/guides/routing-ddr), [autorouting phase API](https://docs.tscircuit.com/elements/autoroutingphase) and [breakout API](https://docs.tscircuit.com/elements/breakout) were read and applied. The user's `lane_bus` refers to the documented `bus_lanes` name.

## Implementation

The two packages are individual JLCPCB imports, corrected against primary ball maps. Every one of the 420 physical balls remains present. There are 49 numeric-pin trace connections: 22 byte signals, 26 synchronous address/command/clock signals and one asynchronous reset. Byte0, byte1 and command/clock have independent buses on L3, L7 and L5. DQS0, DQS1 and CK have explicit differential-pair constraints.

The active `am3352-powered-host.circuit.tsx` uses four native DDR routing phases followed by a power/control phase. The isolated `am3352-ddr-clearance-bootstrap.circuit.tsx` retains the same DDR routing approach. Local pad-to-layer dogbones are generated by the DDR preset; the interconnect then stays on its assigned signal layer. Separate manual-fanout experiments preserve the autorouter for the channel. An opposing-boundary experiment reserves distinct bottom/top exit regions for the two bytes and command bus, with larger fanout areas.

The documented phase configuration is `autorouter="bus_lanes"`; `routingPhaseIndex` on each named electrical trace assigns it to that pass. Buses group those traces but do not create their electrical connections. The preset preserves prior fanout copper, includes its planar lengths in matching, and adds no intermediate channel vias. `pcbAllowedLayers` and `preferredLayers` restrict allowed layers. Supplied fanouts therefore need compatible layer exits and a winding order that permits a planar connection.

For manual local changes, the supported interface is `<breakout pcbTracePaths={paths}>` (also available on its `<fanout>` alias). Saved paths are keyed by port selector and must cover every routing connection owned by that breakout. The global channel still runs `bus_lanes`. A later `reroute` phase can target earlier routed connections or a rectangular region; this does not waive connectivity, clearance, or timing checks. Supply escapes need explicit connections and plane mapping through `fanoutPourNetMap`, rather than overlaying the guide's separate power-dogbone example.

The guide's Winbond example has 47 signals. Our selected Micron 4 Gb x16 part has 49, including A13 and A14; its map must not be replaced by the example's map. Its M7 is NC. All CPU/RAM terminals are independently checked against the selected primary ball maps.
